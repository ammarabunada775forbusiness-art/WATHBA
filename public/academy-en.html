Learn calisthenics, one step at a time

Start with useful movement patterns: push, pull, train your legs and build trunk control. These guides help you learn the exercise before you choose the equipment.

Beginner to intermediateTrain at homeBased in Amman, Jordan

Where should I start?

Advanced skills are not a prerequisite for calisthenics. Choose a route that fits your current capacity and make the movement harder when you can control it well.

Pulling and hanging

Build grip and back strength gradually: try a supported hang, horizontal row or assisted pull-up, then a full rep when you control the lowering phase. Monkey bars and wall ladders offer different movement options depending on the installation. Check the mounting and training space first.

Push-ups, dips and parallel bars

Start with a push-up variation you can control, such as an incline push-up. Full dips put a greater demand on your shoulders: approach them gradually with a stable installation, adequate clearance and no pain. Low bars also support some core and support drills.

Rings: strength and control

Rings move beneath you, so some exercises are harder than their fixed-bar counterparts. Start with a ring row with your feet on the floor, adjusting the body angle. Progress to support holds once you have the capacity and a dependable overhead attachment.

Grip and accessories

Grip improves with progressive hanging and pulling. A hand gripper can complement your training, and grip tape may change how a handle feels. Neither replaces back training or correct equipment installation. Reduce training if your fingers or forearms become irritated.

Planning a training space

Measure the floor area, ceiling height and wall type before ordering a custom rig. Decide whether you want pulls, dips, rings or movement between stations, and allow space around each movement. Ask about the actual equipment and installation requirements instead of judging suitability by a single dimension.

Before you begin

Do I need equipment to start?

You can start with incline push-ups, squats and trunk work. Add a safe pulling setup when one is available.

Is any one product only for advanced athletes?

Many products support several skill levels. The movement, attachment and available space determine the right progression.
