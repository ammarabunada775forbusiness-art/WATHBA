How to choose and train with Steel Parallettes with Wooden Grip

Steel-base parallettes with wooden grips for pushing, support holds and L-sits. This option combines a steel base with a wooden grip as described in the catalog. Use an even floor for push-ups and support holds. Bend the knees before attempting an L-sit; save planche and handstand work for suitable preparation.

Who is it for, and what does it train?

For separate push-up and support handles with several size options. Beginners can keep their feet down; skill athletes should select a base and height for their movement rather than an “advanced” label.

Muscles and movements

Push-ups work the chest, triceps and front shoulders with trunk bracing. Supports and L-sits add shoulder stability, abdominal and hip-flexor demands. A neutral grip changes wrist position but does not guarantee pain relief.

Skills to build

Build push-ups and support first, then tuck holds and single-leg L-sit extensions; handstands need a separate setup and exit practice.

Choose a level that suits your ability; do not combine all three into one session. Counts are adjustable starting examples, not level tests or promised results.

Example starting session

  1. Check floor contact and grip, then gently warm wrists and shoulders without sudden loading.
  2. Start with 3 sets of 5–8 supported push-ups, resting 60–90 seconds.
  3. Add 2 ten-second supports with feet down; replace them with short tuck holds once foot lift is controlled.

Change one variable: reduce knee support, add a little hold time or extend a leg further. Handle or base movement calls for a setup check, not extra reps.

For healthy adults: regress if control fades and stop for pain. Leave a rep or two available rather than forcing every set to failure, and rest longer when needed to restore technique.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Placing handles too wide and forcing an uncomfortable shoulder position.
  • Buying the largest size without checking handle height and base depth.
  • Trying planche or handstand work before learning support and a controlled exit.

Before use: setup, stability and care

  • Use level, nonslip ground and inspect each wood-to-base connection.
  • Check handle cracks or rotation and the floor-contact points before loading.
  • Do not use the pair as jumping steps or assume the height approves swinging movements.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need the largest version for advanced training?

Not necessarily. Choose dimensions for your movement and space, then progress the exercise itself.

Are parallettes enough to learn an L-sit?

They provide support, but the skill still needs pushing strength, trunk control and leg lift. Start with bent knees; height alone does not solve the movement.

Are they better than floor push-ups?

They offer a different grip and floor clearance. Prefer the option you can progress comfortably on; extra height is not a reason to force deeper reps.

Do I need separate pairs for different levels?

Not necessarily. Assistance, leverage and range change exercise difficulty; dimensions should fit use, stability and space.
