Model size and assembled height

How to choose and train with Small Freestanding Parallel Bars

Compact 40 cm freestanding parallel bars for support, push and core drills. The listed height is 40 cm. This can suit support drills, push-ups and knee raises, with range depending on the athlete. Start with foot-assisted support before deeper dips and check both bars for stability.

Who is it for, and what does it train?

For a low support station you can reposition, keeping feet near the floor. The listed 40 cm height can suit push and core drills but may not provide full dip clearance for your body.

Muscles and movements

Supports and pushing involve the triceps, chest and shoulders; knee lifts require trunk control and hip-flexor action. Foot assistance changes how much load you take.

Skills to build

Control a low support and gradually lift the feet, checking bar spacing first.

Exercises for your current ability

Choose a level that suits your ability; do not combine all three into one session. Counts are adjustable starting examples, not level tests or promised results.

Lift one leg without rocking the bases or dragging them inward.

Advanced

Low tuck hold

3 sets × 5–10 seconds

Lift both feet only with enough clearance and stability, then set them down gently.

Example starting session

  1. Set comfortable bar spacing and move furniture away from the knee path.
  2. Do 3 ten-second assisted supports, then 2 sets of 4 knee lifts per side.
  3. Rest 60–90 seconds between sets. If the bases do not stay still, stop and correct the surface and setup.

Reduce foot assistance before extending holds. Never compensate for limited height by stacking blocks under the bars or placing them on furniture.

For healthy adults: regress if control fades and stop for pain. Leave a rep or two available rather than forcing every set to failure, and rest longer when needed to restore technique.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming 40 cm gives every athlete full dip clearance.
  • Bringing bars closer without checking each base’s balance.
  • Dropping quickly or dragging feet in a way that shifts the pair.

Before use: setup, stability and care

  • Use level ground with complete base contact before loading.
  • Check grip width and elbow and knee paths in the actual space.
  • Ask about permitted use before adding straps or sideways movements.

Frequently asked questions

Can I do a full dip on the small version?

That depends on the bar dimensions, your height and the range you need. Confirm measurements; the taller model may better fit full-height dips.

Should I choose small bars or parallettes?

Compare handle height, length and base depth with your drill. Both can support pushing, but body clearance and stability can differ.

Can I raise it myself?

Do not add improvised supports. Request a design for the required height or compare the taller model.

Does smaller mean easier?

Floor proximity may help foot assistance but can make some holds harder through limited clearance. Movement and assistance determine difficulty.
