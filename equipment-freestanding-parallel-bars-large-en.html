Specifications and ordering

These are design reference dimensions; final item dimensions and configuration are confirmed before an order is agreed.

Model size and assembled height
The 100 cm model name refers to the tube frame; reference assembled height is 103.6 cm before rubber feet, rather than 100 cm overall.
Reference footprint per unit
Base depth 55 cm; outside span between the legs 84 cm. Leg centre spacing is 80 cm, not spacing between the two bars.
Materials and grip
Steel bases with a 6 × 3 cm section and 4 cm outside-diameter tubes. Supplied as two independent units.
Reference finish
Matte black steel; other colours require confirmation.
Setup
Two independent units on a level, non-slip floor; inspect connections and base contact before use.
Steel wall thickness
Tube and pipe wall thickness: 2 mm; plate and socket thicknesses are specified separately.

Item weight and load rating are not currently published; a load rating requires documented testing of the actual item and its mounting.

How to choose and train with Large Freestanding Parallel Bars

100 cm freestanding parallel bars for full-height dips and core training. Two freestanding bars at a listed height of 100 cm provide more vertical room than the small version for dips and support work. First develop shoulder control and a comfortable movement range; reduce effort if pain appears.

Who is it for, and what does it train?

For more vertical space for supports and dips, with sufficient floor area for the bases. Beginners can start with assisted support when safe access is possible; a full dip is not a first-session requirement.

Muscles and movements

Dips load the triceps, chest and front shoulders while the shoulder girdle and trunk stabilize the body. Torso angle and range change demands without completely isolating one muscle.

Skills to build

Build steady support, then comfortable elbow-bend range, followed by controlled dips and knee raises without swinging.

Exercises for your current ability

Choose a level that suits your ability; do not combine all three into one session. Counts are adjustable starting examples, not level tests or promised results.

Beginner

Assisted top support

3 sets × 10–20 seconds

Use suitable stable access rather than jumping above the handles.

Intermediate

Controlled-range dip

3 sets × 3–6 reps

Choose comfortable shoulder depth and keep the bases stationary.

Advanced

Slow-lowering dip

3 sets × 3–5 reps

Lower for about 3 seconds without bouncing; do not add weights unapproved for the setup.

Example starting session

  1. Check spacing, height and access, then warm shoulders with an easy floor push variation.
  2. Beginner: 3 assisted supports of 10–15 seconds, resting 90 seconds. Skip dips if support control is lost.
  3. When ready, replace supports with 3 sets of 3–5 assisted dips, then 2 sets of 4–6 knee raises if body control remains.

Progress control, range and assistance before volume. Include pulling and legs in the wider program rather than repeating hard dips every day.

For healthy adults: regress if control fades and stop for pain. Leave a rep or two available rather than forcing every set to failure, and rest longer when needed to restore technique.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Going beyond comfortable shoulder depth just to count a rep.
  • Relaxing and bouncing out of the bottom.
  • Swinging or shifting sideways on bases not designed for it.

Before use: setup, stability and care

  • The listed height is 100 cm; verify actual height, handle length and base depth before ordering.
  • Inspect the floor, joints and rocking, and leave a clear descent path.
  • Do not hang rings or bands from the bars without approval for that loading.

Frequently asked questions

How does it compare with the small version?

The listed height is 100 cm here versus 40 cm for the small bars. Your movement and actual measurements determine the right choice.

Must a dip go below a fixed angle?

Do not force one depth on everyone. Use a controlled, pain-free range and expand it gradually if appropriate.

Can I add weights?

No load approval for that use is stated here. Confirm the design, installation and loading type with WATHBA first.

Does it need floor anchoring?

It is listed as freestanding, but stability and use requirements must be checked for the actual item; avoid improvised anchoring.
