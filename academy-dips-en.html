Parallel Bar Dips: From Support Holds to Your First Rep

Prepared by WATHBA Academy

A dip lowers the body between two handles and presses it back into support. Learning begins with steady support and elbow and shoulder control, rather than the deepest possible descent. Assistance can bridge the gap to a full rep.

Pushing strengthBeginner to intermediateFixed parallel bars

What does a dip train?

The triceps, chest and front shoulders contribute to pressing, while the trunk and shoulder girdle stabilize the body. No single torso angle completely isolates a muscle, and extra depth is not automatically a better rep.

Fixed bars usually offer a simpler starting point than moving rings. Handle height changes body clearance and access, rather than assigning an athlete’s level.

How do you perform it?

Use stable access to the handles and comfortable grip spacing. Press down into support without a painful shoulder shrug. Your feet can carry part of the load.

Bend the elbows slowly and descend only through a comfortable range you can reverse. Keep a repeatable path, press up without kicking or bouncing, then place the feet on stable support rather than releasing the hands.

When should I try a full dip?

When support and assisted elbow bends stay steady, reduce foot assistance a little. Keep the same range at first; changing depth and assistance together makes the source of difficulty unclear.

Lowering-only reps can be more demanding than they appear. Avoid them if you cannot stop the descent or return to stable support. Pain is not a necessary sign of progress.

Examples to match your ability

These examples can be reduced. Choose a stage or drill for your ability and setup; do not combine every row into one session.

Example session and progression

After warming up and checking access, choose one table stage. Start with support if dips are not controlled, resting around 90–120 seconds or longer between sets.

Include pulling, legs and trunk work across the week. If pushing twice, start on nonconsecutive days and reduce volume if recovery suffers. This is an educational example rather than a mandatory level test.

Common mistakes

  • Trying ring dips before mastering ring support.
  • Forcing painful depth or losing control at the bottom.
  • Using excessive spacing or moving bases.

Frequently asked questions

Are low bars enough?

It can support holds and some pushing, but dip clearance depends on bar height and your body. Never raise the equipment on improvised blocks.

Do I need added weights?

Not to start. Learn support and range first; added weight needs equipment intended for that loading.

What if my shoulder hurts?

Stop the painful movement and review range and setup. Persistent pain or injury needs appropriate assessment rather than more reps.
