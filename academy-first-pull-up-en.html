How do I get my first pull-up?

A pull-up requires pulling strength, grip and trunk control. Train manageable parts of the movement first, then build toward a full repetition.

Beginner to intermediatePull-up barProgression guide

What does a pull-up look like?

Use a comfortable overhand grip near shoulder width, brace your body and pull with control until your chin passes the bar if your strength allows. Lower with control to extended arms. Swinging is not needed for your first clean rep. An underhand chin-up shifts the effort and may feel easier for some people.

Check the bar and its attachment before hanging. Reduce the load and seek professional advice if you experience shoulder or elbow pain.

A step-by-step progression

StepHowExample start
Supported hangKeep your feet on the floor or a stable platform2 × 10–20 seconds
Scapular movementWith nearly straight arms, draw the shoulders down slightly without swinging2 × 5–8 reps
Row or assisted pull-upUse foot support or a securely attached assistance band2–3 × 3–6 reps
Controlled negativeStep to the top from a stable platform, then lower as slowly as you can control2 × 2–4 reps
Full pull-upAfter warming up, try one clean rep without swinging1–3 quality attempts

Choose a step you can control; you do not have to perform them all in one workout. Assistance bands are optional rather than essential.

Train at your current level

01 / Beginner

Build a base

Supported hangs 2 × 10–20 seconds, scapular movement 2 × 5–8, and assisted pulls 2 × 3–6.

02 / Intermediate

Clean repetitions

After a warm-up, try 2–3 controlled sets of pull-ups. Use assistance for extra work if needed.

03 / Advanced

Strength and control

Progress tempo and grip gradually. Dynamic skills require adequate clearance, a stable setup and suitable technique.

These are examples; adjust volume to your strength and recovery.

Choosing a wall bar in Jordan

Measure overhead and forward clearance, inspect your wall and ask about the specific installation and rated load. A 60 or 90 cm projection on a WATHBA product describes distance from the wall, not an athlete's level or a guarantee that any particular skill will fit. Match the bar and mounting to your actual training space.

Pull-up questions

Can a beginner use a pull-up bar?

Yes, for supported hangs and assisted pulls even before a first full repetition, provided the installation is sound.

Do I need to master a pull-up before using parallettes?

No. Parallettes focus mainly on pushing and core control; they are a different movement path.

Is a 90 cm projection only for skills?

No. Projection alone does not determine skill suitability; clearance, height, design and mounting matter too.
