How to choose and train with Full Wooden Parallettes

All-wood parallettes for controlled push-ups and bodyweight support drills. The catalog lists an all-wood pair in one standard size. Use them for push-ups, support holds and gradually harder leg lifts. Check the floor and handle stability before loading your weight; skill difficulty does not come from the material alone.

Who is it for, and what does it train?

For athletes who prefer a wooden pair near the floor for pushing and control. Lower clearance can make lifting feet in an L-sit harder; this does not assign it to one experience level.

Muscles and movements

Chest, triceps and shoulders contribute to push-ups; support adds shoulder and trunk stability. Lifting the feet also involves the hip flexors, so the L-sit is more than an isolated abdominal drill.

Skills to build

Low supports and controlled push-ups, followed by single-foot lifts and tucks. Stable support comes before balance practice.

Exercises for your current ability

Choose a level that suits your ability; do not combine all three into one session. Counts are adjustable starting examples, not level tests or promised results.

Keep the body braced as one unit; return to knee support if this breaks down.

Advanced

Single-foot lift from low support

3 sets × 3–5 lifts per side

Lift one foot without pulling the handle or holding your breath; combine feet later when controlled.

Example starting session

  1. Set the pair parallel, check for rocking and clean the base contact surfaces.
  2. Perform 2 sets of 6–8 knee-supported push-ups with 60–90 seconds rest.
  3. Add 3 ten-second feet-down supports. In later sessions, replace one with a single-foot lift.

Do not force a full L-sit while your feet scrape the floor. Bend the knees, build control and reconsider actual clearance if your goal needs more height.

For healthy adults: regress if control fades and stop for pain. Leave a rep or two available rather than forcing every set to failure, and rest longer when needed to restore technique.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Loading the handle edge instead of centering the hand.
  • Assuming wood does not need joint and crack checks.
  • Storing the pair wet and using it without checking the surface and stability.

Before use: setup, stability and care

  • Inspect joints, cracks and handle movement before each session.
  • Follow the supplied wood-care instructions; do not add oil that leaves the grip slippery.
  • Ask about outdoor storage; wooden appearance does not establish weather resistance.

Frequently asked questions

How does this differ from steel-and-wood?

This is the listed all-wood standard-size option. The other has a steel base and three listed sizes. Confirm each product's measurements before choosing.

Do wooden parallettes hold more than steel ones?

Material names cannot rank load capacity. The actual design, joints and verified testing matter; no unverified number is given.

Can they replace tall parallel bars for dips?

Low height limits body clearance. Use appropriate push and support drills, and compare taller bars if dips need more space.

Does a wooden grip treat wrist pain?

No. Grip position can change, but persistent pain calls for appropriate assessment rather than more loading of the painful movement.
